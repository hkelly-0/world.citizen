## History

The Gambia River was a major route for the slave trade, and Britain and France competed for the region. Britain colonized it, and it became independent in 1965. Dawda Jawara led it until a 1994 coup by Yahya Jammeh, who ruled harshly until he lost an election in 2016 and was forced out by regional troops.

## People

The Gambia is mainland Africa's smallest country, a narrow strip along the Gambia River surrounded by Senegal. Most people are Sunni Muslim, from groups such as the Mandinka, Fula, and Wolof. English is official.

## Economy

The Gambia's economy relies on agriculture, especially groundnuts, tourism, and money sent home by Gambians abroad. Many young people try to migrate to Europe.

## International Relations

The Gambia is a member of the Commonwealth, which it rejoined in 2018, and ECOWAS. It has close ties with Senegal, and brought a genocide case against Myanmar to the International Court of Justice in 2019.
