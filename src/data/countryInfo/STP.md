## History

São Tomé and Príncipe was uninhabited until the Portuguese arrived in the 1470s. It became one of the world's first sugar plantation economies, and a hub for the slave trade. Later, cocoa plantations used forced labour. It became independent in 1975 as a one-party state, and held multiparty elections from 1991.

## People

São Tomé and Príncipe is Africa's second-smallest country, made up of two volcanic islands. Most people are of African or mixed descent, and most are Catholic. Portuguese is official, and Creole languages are also spoken.

## Economy

The economy relies on cocoa, tourism, and foreign aid. Hopes of offshore oil wealth have not yet been realized. Much of its food must be imported.

## International Relations

São Tomé and Príncipe is a member of the Community of Portuguese Language Countries and has close ties with Portugal. It has had peaceful changes of government, though there were attempted coups.
