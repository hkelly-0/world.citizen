## History

North America is home to hundreds of Indigenous nations, whose populations and cultures were greatly diminished by European colonization. In 1776, thirteen British colonies declared independence and went on to form the United States. The nation expanded westward, while slavery, which relied on the forced labour of millions of enslaved Africans, continued until it was abolished after the Civil War (1861–65).

## People

The US is the world's third-most populous country, with large white, Hispanic, Black, and Asian communities. English is dominant, with Spanish widely spoken. Most people are Christian, mainly Protestant or Catholic, though a growing share are non-religious.

## Economy

The US has the world's largest economy, led by services, technology, finance, and healthcare, with major manufacturing, energy, and agriculture sectors. The US dollar is the world's main reserve currency, and New York and Silicon Valley are global business hubs.

## International Relations

The US emerged from World War II as a global superpower, and led the West against the Soviet Union in the Cold War. It helped overthrow governments in Iran, Guatemala, and Chile, and after 2001 invaded Afghanistan and Iraq. Today it is the world's leading military power, allied with NATO and countries across Asia.
