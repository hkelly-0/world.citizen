## History

Tanzania's coast was a centre of Swahili trade, and Zanzibar was ruled by Omani sultans and became a hub for the slave trade. Germany and then Britain ruled the mainland. Tanganyika became independent in 1961 under Julius Nyerere, and united with Zanzibar in 1964. Nyerere promoted African socialism and national unity.

## People

Tanzania has over 120 ethnic groups, but Swahili unites them, and ethnic tensions are relatively low. Christianity and Islam are both common, and Zanzibar is mostly Muslim. Swahili and English are official.

## Economy

Tanzania's economy relies on agriculture, gold mining, and tourism to Mount Kilimanjaro, the Serengeti, and Zanzibar. It has grown steadily, though poverty remains widespread.

## International Relations

Tanzania is a member of the Commonwealth and the East African Community, and supported liberation movements across southern Africa. The ruling party has held power since independence, and the 2025 election was marked by deadly protests.
