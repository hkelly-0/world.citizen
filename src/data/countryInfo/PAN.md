## History

Panama was colonized by Spain and joined Colombia after independence in 1821. With US support, it broke away in 1903, and the US then built the Panama Canal, which opened in 1914. The US invaded in 1989 to remove the dictator Manuel Noriega, and handed the canal to Panama in 1999.

## People

Most Panamanians are mestizo and speak Spanish, with Afro-Panamanian and Indigenous communities such as the Guna and Ngäbe. About half the population lives in and around Panama City.

## Economy

Panama has one of the fastest-growing economies in Latin America, centred on the canal, shipping, banking, and trade. It uses the US dollar. Its wealth is unevenly shared, with much poverty in rural and Indigenous areas.

## International Relations

The Panama Canal, a major route for world trade, makes Panama strategically important, especially to the US and China. Panama stresses the canal's neutrality. It has a large ship registry, and its banks have faced scrutiny for secrecy.
