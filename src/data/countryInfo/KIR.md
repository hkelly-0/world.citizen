## History

Kiribati's islands were settled by Micronesians thousands of years ago. Britain ruled them as the Gilbert Islands, and they saw fierce fighting in World War II at Tarawa. Kiribati became independent in 1979. Britain mined Banaba island for phosphate, leaving it largely uninhabitable.

## People

Most people are I-Kiribati and Christian. About half live on South Tarawa, which is densely crowded. English is official, and Gilbertese is widely spoken.

## Economy

Kiribati is one of the poorest Pacific countries, relying on fishing licences, copra, and aid.

## International Relations

Kiribati's islands spread across the equator and the International Date Line, which it moved in 1995 to keep the country on one day. It is threatened by rising seas, and switched recognition from Taiwan to China in 2019.
